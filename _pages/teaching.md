  <h2>Teaching Philosophy</h2>

  <p>
    My goal as an instructor is to make mathematics rigorous without making it inaccessible. Students should learn how to carry out a calculation, but they should also understand why the method works, what assumptions it depends on, and how to recognize when it is useful.
  </p>

  <p>
    I approach difficult topics by breaking abstract ideas into clear steps, connecting formal definitions to visual or physical intuition, and giving students repeated opportunities to explain their own reasoning. I want students to become comfortable asking questions, testing their understanding, and treating mistakes as part of the learning process.
  </p>

  <p>
    Having completed much of my graduate education as a nontraditional adult student, I understand that students arrive in mathematics courses with different levels of preparation, confidence, and prior experience. This perspective has made me especially attentive to the places where students commonly become confused and to the importance of explaining the same idea in more than one way.
  </p>

  <p>
    Above all, I want students to see mathematics as more than a collection of formulas. Mathematics is a rigorous framework for reasoning about problems, evaluating evidence, constructing models, and understanding the natural and technological world.
  </p>

  <h2>Connecting Mathematics to Applications</h2>

  <p>
    My research and professional experience allow me to show students how mathematical ideas are used outside the classroom. I have applied mathematics and statistics to problems involving physical systems, optimal control, remote sensing, physiological data, scientific imaging, finance, and engineering.
  </p>

  <p>
    These experiences provide examples that can help students understand why topics such as differential equations, linear algebra, probability, optimization, and numerical methods matter. They also allow me to discuss the limitations of mathematical models, the role of assumptions, and the difference between obtaining an answer and obtaining an answer that is scientifically meaningful.
  </p>

  <p>
    I am especially interested in supporting students who want to apply mathematics to the life sciences, earth sciences, physics, engineering, environmental research, and other interdisciplinary fields.
  </p>

  <p>
    In professional research roles, I have mentored data scientists and explained complex mathematical and statistical concepts to researchers, engineers, and other collaborators. These experiences strengthened my ability to meet people at their current level of understanding and help them move toward greater independence.
  </p>
