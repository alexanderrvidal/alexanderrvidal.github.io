Hi, I’m Alex — an applied mathematician, data scientist, educator, and scientific consultant.

I use mathematics, statistics, machine learning, optimization, and scientific computing to solve quantitative problems across science, engineering, and industry. I am particularly interested in work involving mathematical modeling, statistical learning, inverse problems, scientific machine learning, and data-driven decision making.

I hold a Ph.D. in Applied Mathematics and Statistics from the Colorado School of Mines. My background combines mathematical research, scientific modeling, university teaching, and several years of professional experience as a data scientist and machine learning researcher.

I am currently open to a range of opportunities, including scientific and quantitative research, data science and machine learning roles, teaching, and selective consulting. I am especially drawn to problems where careful mathematical or statistical reasoning matters and where quantitative work can have a meaningful scientific, technical, or societal impact.

Professional Experience

I have several years of industry experience in data science and machine learning, with work spanning statistical modeling, experimentation, recommendation systems, optimization, ranking, and quantitative decision systems.

At On The Barrelhead and later NerdWallet, I developed and evaluated statistical and machine learning systems used to improve product and business decisions. My work included experimentation, recommendation and ranking, optimization, and translating quantitative analyses into practical product decisions.

I subsequently worked as a Senior Machine Learning Researcher at Launch Potato, where I served as a company-wide technical resource for statistical modeling, experimentation, optimization, and machine learning research. My work included recommender systems, ranking and calibration methods, bandit approaches, and optimization of data-driven decision systems.

These roles gave me experience working across the full lifecycle of quantitative projects: framing ambiguous problems, developing models, designing experiments, evaluating performance, communicating results, and collaborating with engineering and product teams to put useful methods into practice.

Current Scientific Consulting

I currently consult with Denver Life Sciences on statistical and mathematical modeling of physiological study data. My work includes structured and regularized regression methods for distinguishing stable baseline differences among experiments from changes that occur within experiments over time.

The broader goal is to improve prediction and help researchers interpret physiological measurements in the context of tissue hydration. This work combines statistical estimation, mathematical modeling, model validation, and close collaboration with domain experts.

Research Background

I completed my Ph.D. in Applied Mathematics and Statistics at the Colorado School of Mines, where my dissertation examined deep learning methods for large-scale problems in physics. My research brought together ideas from optimal transport, optimal control, inverse problems, numerical optimization, statistical learning, and scientific computing.

My graduate research included:

  • Inverse problems and scientific imaging: using hyperspectral remote-sensing data to estimate mineral composition and support geologic interpretation.
  • High-dimensional scientific computing: creating scalable approximation and optimization methods for problems that are difficult to solve using conventional numerical techniques.
  • Optimal transport and generative modeling: developing methods that connect continuous normalizing flows with Wasserstein gradient flows.
  • Mean-field optimal control: designing computational methods for controlling large interacting systems, including simulated swarms of autonomous agents.

These projects taught me how to formulate mathematical questions, develop computational methods, evaluate their limitations, and communicate results across disciplinary boundaries. My publications are available through the Google Scholar link in the sidebar.
